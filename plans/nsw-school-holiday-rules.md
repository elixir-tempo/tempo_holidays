# NSW school holiday rules

**Status:** deferred, 2026-09-30

The module shipped (2026-09-29); its open questions are parked, as the user judges it not in good shape for its purpose as a Tempo showcase.

The NSW Department of Education sets public school terms and publishes them a few years ahead, but it publishes dates, not rules. This document records the rules that reproduce every published term date from 2016 to 2030, for both divisions, and plans a tempo_holidays module that generates school terms and holidays from them. It replaces a draft whose rules, generated by Gemini, were mostly wrong (see [The Gemini draft, checked](#the-gemini-draft-checked)).

## Sources

* **Term dates** — [Future and past NSW term dates and school holidays](https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/calendars/future-and-past-nsw-term-and-vacation-dates), 2016–2025 and 2027–2030, with school development days included in each term, read 2026-09-29.

* **2026 and 2027** — the [2026](https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/calendars/2026) and [2027](https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/calendars/2027) pages, which list students' first and last days and the school development days separately.

* **School development days** — published for 2023 onwards, on the term-dates page and the [school development days](https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/calendars/school-development-days) page.

* **Public holidays** — [NSW public holidays](https://www.nsw.gov.au/about-nsw/public-holidays), which gives an additional day for Anzac Day on a weekend in 2026 and 2027.

The Department's pages on [school development days](https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/calendars/school-development-days) and [Western division schools](https://education.nsw.gov.au/schooling/calendars/late-start-schools) say only that the dates "are determined by the department". The Teachers Handbook's chapters on conditions of employment and on leave describe the four terms and four student vacations, and annual leave taken in each vacation's first week, but no rule for dating them.

## The rules

A school day is a weekday in a term that is not a public holiday; school development days are school days.

* **Term 1 ends before Easter or in the week of 7–13 April** — on the day before Good Friday when Good Friday falls 31 March–13 April, so Easter opens the autumn holidays (2020, 2021, 2023, 2026), and otherwise on the Friday that falls 7–13 April.

* **The autumn, winter and spring holidays are two weeks** — each runs from the Monday after a term's last day, and the next term starts on the Monday two weeks later, or on the next school day when that Monday is a public holiday: Anzac Day in 2016 and 2022, its additional day in 2027, Labour Day in 2021.

* **Term 3 is ten weeks** — Monday of its first week to Friday of its tenth, every year.

* **Term 2 runs to Term 3** — up to 2025 Term 2 is ten weeks. From 2026 Term 3 starts on the Monday on or after 18 July, so Term 2 is ten weeks or eleven (2026 and 2028).

* **Term 4 ends in the week before Christmas** — up to 2025, on the Friday that falls 17–23 December, or on that week's Tuesday when the Friday is the 22nd or 23rd. From 2026, on the Thursday that falls 17–23 December, or on that week's Monday when the Thursday is the 22nd or 23rd (2027).

* **Term 1 starts after Australia Day** — up to 2021, on the school day after Australia Day (its Monday when 26 January falls on a weekend). From 2022 it starts six weeks after the previous Term 4 ended, on the same weekday, unless the year would then have fewer than 199 school days, when it starts on the school day after Australia Day (2023, 2026, 2029).

* **The Western division starts a week later** — its Term 1 starts seven days after the Eastern division's, so its summer holidays end a week later; every other date is shared.

The rules run in the order that leaves each one's inputs known: Easter places Term 1's end, the breaks and Term 3 follow, December places Term 4's end, and Term 1's start comes last because the six-week rule counts the whole year's school days.

| From | Term 1 starts | Term 2 | Term 4 ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | After Australia Day | Ten weeks | A Friday, or a Tuesday |
| 2022 | Six weeks after Term 4, or after Australia Day | Ten weeks | A Friday, or a Tuesday |
| 2026 | Six weeks after Term 4, or after Australia Day | To Term 3 on or after 18 July | A Thursday, or a Monday |

## School development days

* **2023 and 2024** — the first two school days of Term 1, the first of Terms 2 and 3, and the last two of Term 4.

* **2025 onwards** — the first four school days of Term 1, the first two of Term 2, and the first of Terms 3 and 4. In 2027 Term 2's are its Tuesday and Wednesday, after the Anzac Day additional day.

Before 2023 the Department's pages do not list them, and 2028–2030's are not yet published.

## Public holidays

The rules need Australia Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, Anzac Day, the King's Birthday and Labour Day. When Anzac Day falls on a weekend NSW has an additional public holiday on the Monday from 2026. Before 2026 there was none for a Saturday: Term 2 of 2020 started on Monday 27 April 2020.

Reading NSW's holidays from tempo_holidays turned up two defects, both Open items in [TODO.md](../TODO.md):

* **Anzac Day before 2026** — the date-holidays data gives NSW an additional day for a Saturday Anzac Day before 2026 (`04-25 and if saturday then next monday`, active until 2026-04-25), making Monday 27 April 2020 a holiday. The 2020 school calendar says it was not: an upstream data error, which puts Term 2 of 2020 a day late until it is corrected.

* **Anzac Day listed twice** — `Tempo.Holidays.holidays(:AU, subdivision: "NSW")` lists Anzac Day twice on 25 April in every year it falls on a weekday, from the two dated rules before and from 2026.

## How well the rules fit

`Tempo.Holidays.SchoolTerms.NSW` reproduces every published term start and end, 2016–2030, in both divisions, every published holiday, and the development days for 2023–2027, except Term 2 of 2020, which the Anzac Day data error below starts a day late. Some of the fit is thin, and the tests say so:

* **The 199-day threshold** — 2027 keeps the six-week start with 199 school days and 2029 falls back with 198, so the threshold is pinned by those two years alone.

* **The Term 4 fallback from 2026** — seen once, in 2027, when the Thursday is the 23rd. A Thursday on the 22nd is inferred from the Tuesday fallback before 2026, which is seen for both the 22nd and the 23rd.

* **Term 3's July anchor** — "the Monday on or after 18 July" and "twenty-one weeks before Term 4's last week" fit 2026–2030 equally. They part when 18 July is a Monday, next in 2033.

* **Term 1 of 2031** — the 2030 calendar ends the summer holidays on Monday 27 January 2031, as though Term 1 of 2031 will start after Australia Day, where the six-week start leaves 199 school days, as in 2027, which kept it. Either the date waits on 2031's calendar, or the threshold is 200 counted as each calendar stood when it was set, before Anzac Day's additional day moved Term 2 of 2027 to a Tuesday.

* **When each change began** — Term 1's six-week start first shows in 2022, since 2021 fits either rule; the four-two-one-one development days start in 2025; Term 4's Thursday end in 2026; and the July anchor could have begun in 2024, which fits it.

A year after the last published calendar is a projection under the current rules, and a year before 2016 is outside what the rules were checked against.

## The module

A module in tempo_holidays that generates a year's school terms, holidays and development days for either division, written as a showcase of Tempo: the rules above should read in the code as they read here, with Tempo values and set algebra doing the work and no date arithmetic by hand. Where a rule cannot be written that way, the missing operation is a Tempo API gap, reported and fixed in Tempo rather than papered over with a helper.

* **Terms** — an interval set of four half-open terms, each tagged with its number, so Term 1 of 2027 is `~o"2027Y1M28D/4M10D"`.

* **Holidays** — the complement of the terms within the school year, the summer holidays running into the next year, and each tagged autumn, winter, spring or summer.

* **Development days** — the days the patterns above give, for 2023 onwards.

* **Division** — `:eastern` by default, `:western` for the late-start schools.

The operations the rules need, each to be checked against Tempo's API as the module is written:

* **The weekday on or after a date** — the Friday that falls 7–13 April, the Monday on or after 18 July.

* **Good Friday** — from Easter, through Tempo's `(easter)e` event or tempo_holidays' own NSW holidays.

* **The next school day** — the next weekday that is not a public holiday, with the holidays as a set.

* **Counting school days** — the weekdays of a set of terms less its public holidays.

* **Weeks** — the Monday of a value's week, and a value six weeks on.

* **Gaps** — the holidays between terms as a set difference.
